HOW SMALL CAN AN IMAGE BECOME?

I started by looking at how low-data images are made: reducing resolution and colour depth, compressing files, and using dithering to suggest missing tones. I wanted to understand how much an image could lose while still remaining recognizable, and what “small enough” might mean in practice.

Component
Research
Status
Transplanted

Low-tech Magazine — How to Build a Low-tech Website?

I looked at how Low-tech Magazine uses reduced-colour, dithered images to lower the weight of its pages. It showed me that losing detail could also become part of a website’s visual language.

Small File Media Festival

Although it focuses on moving images, the festival treats file size as a creative condition. It shifted my question from preserving maximum quality to finding what an image still needs to remain legible and expressive.

ImageMagick — Color Quantization and Dithering

The ImageMagick examples gave me a technical reference for colour reduction and dithering. Comparing its patterns helped me understand how a limited number of colours can still suggest gradients and tonal detail.

Ditherpunk — Surma

This article helped me understand and compare different dithering methods. I began to notice how each algorithm gives the same image a different texture, rhythm, and level of detail.

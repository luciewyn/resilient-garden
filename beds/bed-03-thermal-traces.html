DESCRIPTION

This bed looks at the conditions that keep the garden alive.

Rather than treating infrastructure as something hidden in the background, it asks how hosting, storage, energy, and access can become visible parts of a publication.

The garden is built as a small static site that can be hosted locally, without depending entirely on a distant server. Some parts can run on energy gathered through a portable solar panel and stored in a battery.

Because sunlight is uneven, the garden cannot assume constant abundance. On bright days, there may be enough power for images to move and tools to stay awake. During cloudy periods, the garden may need to slow down: movement is reduced, files become lighter, and some parts rest until more energy returns.

Infrastructure here is not only technical support. It shapes how the publication looks, behaves, and remains accessible over time.

EXPERIMENTS IN THIS BED

01 → STATIC HTML

The garden is made from hand-written HTML and CSS, with JavaScript used only where interaction requires it. Each page is kept small, direct, and readable without external frameworks.

02 → LOCAL HOSTING

I tested hosting the website from a local device and opening it through a shared network. This allows the garden to remain accessible nearby without relying on a continuous internet connection.

03 → MAKING ITS OWN POWER

A portable solar panel was connected to an Anker SOLIX power station to test whether the garden could produce and store some of the energy it needed.

04 → DESIGNING FOR A CLOUDY DAY

Changing weather led to different design responses. Images can become lighter, movement can be reduced, and some tools can remain dormant until more energy is available.

IMAGE REDUCTION

BED 01 ✦ STATUS Growing ✦ DATE PLANTED 14.04.26 ✦ LAST TENDED 09.08.26

METHODS

Downsampling, bit-depth reduction, Floyd–Steinberg error diffusion, Bayer ordered dithering, 1-bit image-making

GROWN HERE

Greenhouse Image Tool
1-bit images for e-ink and thermal print

DESCRIPTION

This bed asks what happens when an image is asked to live with less. The same image is passed through a series of reductions. Its dimensions shrink, its tonal range narrows, and its colours are eventually reduced to black and white. Dithering returns some of the missing detail by rearranging the pixels that remain.

The aim is not to find one correct or smallest image. Each reduction is treated as a visual and editorial decision: what remains legible, what disappears, and what new textures begin to grow when less information is available.

EXPERIMENTS IN THIS BED

01 → DOWNSAMPLING

Reducing the number of pixels changes both the size and structure of an image. Details merge, edges become more visible, and individual pixels begin to shape what can be seen.

02 → REDUCED BIT DEPTH

Limiting the number of available tones removes gradual transitions. The image must carry its information through a much smaller visual vocabulary.

03 → DITHERING

Floyd–Steinberg error diffusion and Bayer ordered dithering use different arrangements of black and white pixels to suggest tones that are no longer available.

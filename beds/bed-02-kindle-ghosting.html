SCREEN TRACES

BED 02 ✦ STATUS Growing ✦ DATE PLANTED 05.05.26 ✦ LAST TENDED 09.08.26

METHODS

E-ink display testing, frame-by-frame image sequencing, page refresh, ghosting, screen observation and photography

GROWN HERE

A frame-by-frame image sequence for an old Kindle
Observations of ghosting, flashing, and delay

DESCRIPTION

This bed follows what an image leaves behind on an old Kindle screen. A sequence of 1-bit images was loaded onto the device and advanced one frame at a time. Instead of changing immediately, the display paused, flashed, and sometimes held faint parts of the previous image.

These traces are usually treated as faults to be removed. Here, they become part of the image. The experiment looks at how the Kindle’s slow refresh changes movement, how one frame remains inside the next, and how the timing of a screen can shape the way an image is read.

The photographs document different moments in the sequence, including page-turn flashes and fragments of earlier frames that remain visible.

EXPERIMENTS IN THIS BED

01 → OPENING THE KINDLE

I tested ways of opening locally stored images on an old Kindle, including an attempt to use KOReader. This was the first step towards treating the device as a small display for my own image sequences.

02 → FRAME BY FRAME

A short image sequence was exported as individual 1-bit frames and viewed one page at a time. The Kindle slowed the movement down, turning the sequence into a series of pauses and transitions.

03 → GHOSTING

During page turns, parts of the previous frame sometimes remained on the screen. These temporary overlaps produced images that were not contained in the original files but appeared through the behaviour of the display itself.

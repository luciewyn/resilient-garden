A webpage can feel light, almost immaterial. Yet even the smallest image arrives through a chain of energy, storage, software, cables and screens.

The Resilient Garden began by paying attention to this chain; what it needs, and how it might become visible within a publication.

The first materials were already close at hand: hand-written HTML, small image files, an old Kindle, a portable solar battery and a thermal printer.

As they were brought together, their limits began to shape the rhythm. Images became smaller. Old screens left traces. Changes in sunlight affected what the garden could do. These conditions became part of its form and rhythm.

Garden Bed 01 — Image Reduction: original, downsampled, reduced tones, dithered.

Inside the Greenhouse, an image is asked to travel lightly. Its resolution is lowered. Colour is reduced. Dithering rebuilds the missing tones from small arrangements of pixels.

The process is partly subtraction and partly translation. Some details fall away. Others become unexpectedly clear. New textures appear where the smooth image used to be.

On the Kindle, slow refresh allows parts of an earlier image to remain. One screen quietly settles on top of another.

The thermal printer translates the same pixels through heat. Fine lines may disappear, while darker areas gather on the surface of the paper. Each device leaves its own reading of the image: a delay, a shadow, a softened edge or an uneven mark.

Some parts of the garden are powered through a portable solar battery. Its rhythm changes with the weather. When energy is plentiful, images can move and tools stay awake. When the sky remains cloudy, the garden begins to slow down.

Files become lighter. Movement is reduced. Some parts wait quietly until more energy arrives. The weather does not stay outside the publication. It enters the way the garden appears and behaves.

Open it out! Unfold the paper to find the garden map on the other side.

Follow the map online, or fold it back and take this small harvest with you. The garden is still growing.
